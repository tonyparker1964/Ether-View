          What's real here

          Music: “The Big Bang · Zero Point Genesis” by Ether. Add your own songs from the music panel; they stay in your browser and the strings and Sun move with them too. The Big Bang simulation runs the length of the song, and in the explorer the strings and the Sun can pulse with its bass.

          Quantum strings are an artistic layer, not a physical one: a vibrating web of light joining every body in the app. The Sun, Moon and planets are all linked to one another; every asteroid, dwarf planet and moon links to its two nearest neighbors; stars, star systems, nebulae, black holes and the Magellanic Clouds form a galactic web; and each exoplanet links to its star. Each body has its own color, or switch to aspect colors to see major astrological aspects. Strings between bodies in aspect pulse. Adjust them with the translucency slider in Layers.

          Asteroids: more than 1.4 million asteroids are known. The 64,000 shown are a model built to match the real population: the main belt with its Kirkwood gaps, the Hungarias, the Hildas, Jupiter’s Trojans sharing its orbit 60° ahead and behind, and near-Earth asteroids, each moving on its own orbit. The 27 named asteroids and centaurs use real orbits; Vesta, Didymos, Itokawa, Apophis, the Trojans Hektor and Patroclus, and Chiron are placed along their orbits from real events, and the rest are placed approximately. The full catalog can be loaded in a desktop build.

          Zodiac: signs are sidereal (Nirayana) by default, measured against the fixed stars with the Lahiri ayanamsa (about 24° today), as in Vedic astrology. Switch to the tropical (Sayana) zodiac of Western astrology in Layers; the two currently differ by about 24°, close to one sign.

          Deep time: the clock runs from the Big Bang, 13.8 billion years ago, to 100 trillion years ahead. Notes under the clock describe the expected state of the Sun and the universe; the planets, stars and galaxy are always drawn as they are today.

          Star systems: distances, sizes and orbit periods are real catalog values; where a planet’s size is unknown it is estimated, and orbit tilts are simplified. Double-sun systems show both stars circling their shared center with the planets circling the pair.

          Constellations are drawn as they appear from the solar system and fade out as you leave it. Planet spin can run at real speed or sped up from the Layers panel.

          Computed for the date shown: positions of the Sun, Moon and planets (approximate orbital elements, good to a fraction of a degree between 1800 and 2050 and increasingly approximate further out; beyond about 3,000 years the orbits are extrapolated from today’s, so deep-time positions are illustrative), the Moon's Earth-facing orientation, Earth's rotation, and the zodiac readings.

          Real catalog values: the planet facts, named stars, nebulae and clusters, the Voyager directions, the Moon's named maria and craters, and the Milky Way's overall dimensions and arm layout.

          Nebula images: the nebulae and Pleiades use credited telescope observations, available in their details panels. Telescope colors combine selected filters and may show enhanced or assigned colors. Cloud depth is illustrative.

          Modeled stand-ins: the close-up lunar terrain, where moons and exoplanets sit along their orbits, black-hole visuals, spacecraft models and orbit phases, background stars and the galaxy's individual particles.
